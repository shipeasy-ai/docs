Connect Slack **once** per project and you unlock every Shipeasy ↔ Slack
surface: new bugs and requests stream into a channel, platform events post in,
you act on them with buttons and dropdowns, and you can drive your whole flags &
experiments platform from Slack — by slash command, by @-mention, or through
Slack's own AI assistant.

## Connect Slack

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the connector">
    On the **Feedback** page open **Connectors → New connector**, pick **Slack**, name it, choose
    the events to forward, and **Continue**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add to Slack">
    You're redirected to authorize the Shipeasy Slack app in your workspace. After consent you land
    back in the configure view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick channels">
    Choose the **feedback channel** new bugs/requests post to. Optionally pick a separate
    **notifications channel** for platform events — leave it on *Same as feedback channel* to share
    one. Tick **Enabled**, **Save**, and use **Send test** to post a throwaway message.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="One connection, every surface">
  The same Slack connection powers all of the features below — feedback, notifications, the buttons,
  the `/shipeasy` command, the `@Shipeasy` assistant, and the Slack-AI connection. Connect once and
  they all light up.
</Callout>

## Bugs & requests in a channel

Every new **bug report** and **feature request** posts to your feedback channel,
with the title, reporter, page URL, and full details (steps / actual / expected
for bugs; description / use-case / priority for requests).

### Status control — a dropdown right on the card

Each feedback card has a **Status ▾** dropdown covering the whole lifecycle:

| Status                  | Border   |
| ----------------------- | -------- |
| Open                    | 🔵 blue  |
| In progress             | 🟠 amber |
| Ready for QA            | 🟠 amber |
| Resolved                | 🟢 green |
| **Won't fix / Dismiss** | ⚪ grey  |

Pick one and the item's status updates in Shipeasy, the card stamps who changed
it, and the **coloured left border** flips to match. **Won't fix / Dismiss is a
distinct grey — never the green of Resolved** — so dropped and done read
differently at a glance. When you later
[link a pull request](/feedback/getting-started), the card is edited to show the
PR link and turns amber (work in flight).

## Platform notifications — and acting on them

Internal platform events post to your **notifications channel**, and most of them
carry **action buttons** so you can respond without leaving Slack. Choose which
events go to Slack in **Settings → Notifications** (the Slack column is live once
a connector exists).

| Event                               | Card actions                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Alert triggered**                 | `Resolve` · `Dismiss` · `Mute rule` (disables the rule that fired) |
| **Experiment reached significance** | `Ship "<group>"` · `Stop experiment` · `Keep running`              |
| **Guardrail breached**              | `Stop experiment` · `Acknowledge`                                  |
| **Kill switch flipped**             | `Flip back` (one-click revert)                                     |
| **Config published to prod**        | `Acknowledge`                                                      |
| **Agent needs your attention**      | `Open the issue ↗` · `Acknowledge`                                 |
| Weekly digest · New team member     | _(informational — link only)_                                      |

Each action writes straight back into Shipeasy, records an audit entry, stamps
who did it, and recolours the card. Alert cards are bordered by severity
(🔴 danger / 🟠 warn / 🔵 info) and turn 🟢 green on resolve or ⚪ grey on dismiss.

<Callout type="info" title="Privileged actions check your membership">
  Stopping or shipping an experiment and flipping a kill switch go through the real admin path (so
  the change propagates), and they verify **you're a member of the project** first — your Slack
  identity is matched to your Shipeasy email. If you're not a member, only you see a quiet "ask an
  admin to invite you" — the shared card is left untouched for everyone else.
</Callout>

### Agent-attention opens the issue

When an unattended agent run gets blocked it raises an **"Agent needs your
attention"** card. Its **Open the issue ↗** button takes you straight to the
specific item it's stuck on (not a generic dashboard landing), where the full
escalation — the summary and the numbered steps the agent left — is shown
in context.

## `/shipeasy` — create an alert from a channel

Type the slash command in any channel to stand up a metric alert whose
notifications post back to **that** channel:

```text
/shipeasy alert <metric> <gt|gte|lt|lte> <threshold> [name]
```

For example `/shipeasy alert api-errors gt 50 high error rate` fires when
`api-errors` exceeds 50 over 24h and posts to the channel you ran it in. The
reply is private to you.

## `@Shipeasy` — run the platform by chat

Mention **@Shipeasy** in a channel and ask in plain language. The assistant can
**read and change** your **feature flags, dynamic configs, kill switches, A/B
experiments, alert rules, and metrics** — the same toolset the dashboard
assistant has:

```text
@Shipeasy roll the new-checkout gate to 25%
@Shipeasy which experiments are significant right now?
@Shipeasy create a kill switch for the payments path
```

It replies in the same thread and tells you what it did.

## Drive Shipeasy from Slack's AI

If you'd rather use **Slack's own AI assistant**, Shipeasy is also available there
as an MCP connection — the same flags, configs, kill switches, experiments, and
metrics. Your workspace admin enables it once under the Slack App's **MCP Server
Connection** (Slack identity auth). After that, ask Slack's assistant to manage
Shipeasy and it calls through to your project.

<Callout type="info" title="Everything acts as you">
  Every "do something" surface — the card buttons, `/shipeasy`, `@Shipeasy`, and the Slack-AI
  connection — acts as **you**: your Slack identity is matched to your Shipeasy email, and you must
  be a **member of the project**. A non-member gets a clear "ask an admin to invite you," never
  silent access.
</Callout>

## Channel routing

- **Feedback channel** — where new bugs and requests land.
- **Notifications channel** — where platform events post (defaults to the feedback channel).
- **Per-alert channel** — an alert created with `/shipeasy` (or with a channel chosen in the
  dashboard) posts to that specific channel, overriding the project default.

## Status & health

The connector in the list shows a status — **Needs OAuth**, **Needs channel**
(target not picked yet), **Active**, **Disabled**, or **Failing** (last dispatch
errored). The configure view surfaces the last error and last successful delivery
time, so you can tell at a glance whether mirroring is healthy.

<Callout type="warn" title="The bot needs your email">
  The `/shipeasy` command, `@Shipeasy` assistant, privileged card actions, and the Slack-AI
  connection resolve access by your Slack email, which needs the `users:read.email` scope. If you
  connected Slack before this was added, click **Add to Slack** again to re-grant — otherwise those
  features can't tell who you are.
</Callout>
